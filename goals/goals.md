# Financial Goals

_Context: 24 years old, founder of a startup. Salary is small and covers essentials but leaves no slack ("paycheck to paycheck"). ~$100,000 invested in Vanguard. No high-interest debt. No kids yet — family/kids are years out, not immediate._

## Short-term (next 1–2 years) — actionable now

The priority right now is building a liquid cushion without touching the Vanguard investment, since that money is working long-term and shouldn't be the emergency fund.

- [ ] **Build a liquid emergency fund** separate from Vanguard. Target: 3–6 months of essential expenses in a Capital One savings account. _(Fill in: monthly essential expenses = $____, so target = $____)_
- [ ] **Track monthly cash flow explicitly** — income vs. essential expenses vs. margin. Even a simple monthly check-in removes the "paycheck to paycheck" uncertainty.
- [ ] **Don't touch the $100k Vanguard balance** for day-to-day cash flow gaps — treat it as untouchable long-term capital, not a safety net.
- [ ] **Check insurance gaps** — as a founder you likely don't have employer-provided health/disability/life insurance. Confirm what you currently have (if anything) and close obvious gaps.
- [ ] **Revisit salary as the startup's cash position improves** — paying yourself more isn't indulgent if the company can support it; it's what builds the emergency fund faster.
- [ ] **Automate even small savings** — even $50–100/month into the emergency fund builds the habit before cash flow improves.

## Long-term (family-oriented)

These are directional since kids/family are years out — revisit and get specific once there's a timeline.

- [ ] **Define a retirement target** — rough age and lifestyle, to back into a savings rate later.
- [ ] **Grow the Vanguard investment steadily** — once short-term cash flow stabilizes, set a recurring contribution (even small and automatic beats sporadic lump sums).
- [ ] **Life insurance before having kids** — term life is cheap at 24–30 and should be in place before a family depends on your income.
- [ ] **529 plan or education savings vehicle** — not urgent yet, but worth opening once there's an actual kid/timeline, since time-in-market matters most for education savings.
- [ ] **Homeownership** — flag as a goal if relevant; add target timeline/down payment number when ready.
- [ ] **Estate basics** (will, beneficiary designations) — do this once you're married/have kids, not before it's needed.
